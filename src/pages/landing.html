How it works

Three steps, and no statistics degree.

You decide what to test. pivitlab works out how long it needs, keeps you honest while it runs, and gives you a straight answer at the end.

1

Set it up

Add one line of code to your site, then change a headline, a button or an image in the editor. You can test up to three versions at once. The planner tells you how many visitors you'll need and roughly how long that will take.

"About 9,800 visitors per version, roughly 2 weeks at your current traffic."
2

It runs to plan

Your visitors are split fairly between the versions. The score stays hidden until the plan is reached, so nobody calls a winner on Tuesday that's gone by Friday. You can see progress and a health check the whole time.

Progress 62% · day 9 of 14
Results hidden until the plan is reached
3

Get an honest verdict

Each version gets a clear Win, Loss or Inconclusive, with a 95% range showing how big the effect could really be. Share it with your team or client in their own portal, or export the numbers as a CSV.

FAQ

Good questions

Do I need a developer?

Usually not. Someone needs to add one line of code to your site once, much like adding Google Analytics. After that you can change text, buttons and images in the editor yourself.

Why can't I see the results while the test is running?

Because peeking is the most common way A/B tests go wrong. Early numbers swing about, and stopping when they look good produces "wins" that aren't real. pivitlab shows progress and a health check while it runs, and reveals the result once the planned number of visitors and the minimum run time are both reached.

What does "Inconclusive" mean?

It means the test didn't find a difference big enough to be confident about. That's still useful: the 95% range tells you how big any effect could realistically be, so you can decide whether to keep the change, try something bolder, or move on.
